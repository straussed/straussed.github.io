Social decision-making, communication, and movement

[PLACEHOLDER: theme image — 16:9]

Hyenas, like humans and a handful of other animals, have remarkably flexible socio-spatial behavior. They can leave and rejoin their group, shift who they spend the day near, and change how far they range—which lets them tune their social environment to get the most out of group living. The same flexibility adds considerable complexity to their social lives: every day brings decisions about where to go, who to go with, and who to signal to.

We examine movement and communication decisions to uncover the heuristics animals use and what those heuristics reveal about the costs and benefits of group living. Because social structure is built out of these decisions, this work grounds the social structures that shape fitness—the hierarchies and alliances of our first theme—in the day-to-day choices animals actually make.

Questions we're asking

What rules do animals use to decide where to go and who to be near?

What is communication doing, moment to moment?

How do individuals manage the trade-offs of group living in real time?

How much do decision rules vary among individuals and across contexts?

How do individual decisions add up to social structure?

What do the rules reveal about the costs and benefits of group living?
