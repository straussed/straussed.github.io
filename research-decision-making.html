Social decision-making, communication, and movement

Spotted hyenas, like humans and a small number of other species, live in fission–fusion societies in which group members associate in subgroups that change composition throughout the day. This flexibility allows individuals to modulate their social and spatial environment, adjusting the costs and benefits of group living on short timescales. It also reshapes the way information flows through social groups and imposes a continual demand for decisions: where to travel, with whom to associate, and when and what to signal.

Social structure accumulates from these decisions, but the heuristics underlying them remain poorly characterized. To deliver a process-based understanding of social influences on animal ecology and evolution, we are decomposing established patterns in social structure into the moment-to-moment movement and communication decisions made by the animals in real time. We examine acoustic information flow to understand how these groups remain connected across large spatial scales, how foraging behavior is influenced by near and distant group-mates, and how social and courtship relationships are maintained despite highly dynamic social and spatial arrangements.

Questions we're asking

What decision rules govern movement and association?

What function does communication serve in coordinating movement and association?

How does information about foraging opportunities flow among distantly spaced individuals?

How do individuals modulate the costs and benefits of group living on short timescales?

Do decision rules vary among individuals and across social contexts?

How do individual decisions scale up to group-level social structure?
