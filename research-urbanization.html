Urbanization and animal societies

[PLACEHOLDER: theme image — 16:9]

Socioecological theory holds that societies are shaped by the ecological conditions in which they evolve. Behavioral plasticity says something different in emphasis: that animals tune their behavior to the conditions they face right now. Urbanization brings the two into collision, remaking ecological conditions—where food is, when it appears, who is dangerous, where it is safe to be—far faster than a society can evolve in response.

We know very little about what happens to animal societies under that kind of rapid change. Cities and their edges are, in effect, an unplanned experiment on the ecological foundations of social life, and we use them to ask which features of a society track current conditions, which are held in place by history, and what it costs animals when the two come apart.

Questions we're asking

Which features of a society respond to urban conditions, and which stay put?

Is what we see plasticity, or evolution?

Do urban resources change what animals compete over, and how?

Does urbanization change who does well?

How do urban risks reshape movement and communication?

What happens when ecological change outpaces social change?
